About the EXIF Inspector

Every photo you take hides a small dossier inside it: the camera model, lens, exposure settings, exact timestamp — and often the GPS coordinates of where you were standing. This EXIF inspector reads that metadata locally and lays it out in plain English. One click later, you can download a metadata-free copy of the same picture.

Sharing photos with location data attached is a genuine privacy risk: it reveals your home, your workplace, your child's school. Journalists, activists, landlords, parents and anyone selling items online should strip metadata first. Because the inspection and removal happen entirely on your device, even highly sensitive photos never leave your hands.

Frequently asked questions

What is EXIF data exactly?

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is metadata embedded by cameras and phones: device model, settings, date/time and often GPS coordinates. It is invisible when viewing the photo but trivially readable by anyone with the file.

Do social networks strip EXIF for me?

Most major platforms do — but email, messaging apps, cloud links, classifieds and forums often do not. Stripping before sharing is the only reliable habit.

Does removing metadata reduce image quality?

No. The pixels are copied as-is; only the metadata block is discarded. The clean copy is visually identical and usually a few kilobytes smaller.

Are my photos uploaded during inspection?

Never. The file is read with the browser File API and parsed by JavaScript running on your device. You can verify this in your browser's network tab — after the page loads, no requests are made.
