About the Video Compressor

Shrink large video files without uploading a single byte. This compressor runs the real FFmpeg engine — compiled to WebAssembly — inside your browser tab. Choose a resolution cap and a quality preset, and the video is re-encoded locally with modern H.264 settings that routinely cut file sizes by 50–80% while staying sharp.

It is built for the moments every online tool fails you: a 400 MB clip that email will not take, a Discord or WhatsApp limit, a course platform with a 100 MB cap. Because the encoding happens on your machine with FFmpeg.wasm, hour-long home videos, screen recordings and drone footage never touch a server — there is no upload progress bar because there is no upload.

Frequently asked questions

How much smaller will my video get?

Typically 50–80% for phone and screen recordings encoded with default settings, depending on source quality and your preset. The tool shows the exact result — and never silently gives you a bigger file without telling you.

Will my video lose quality?

Some, at lower presets — that is how compression works. Crisp preset at 1080p is usually indistinguishable from the source for sharing purposes; Tiny prioritizes size for email and chat limits.

Why is the first compression slower?

The FFmpeg engine (~30 MB) downloads once to your browser cache and stays loaded. Subsequent runs start instantly. Everything after that first load works even offline.

Is my video uploaded to a server?

No — that is the core feature. Encoding runs in your browser via WebAssembly. A 2 GB video can be compressed on a plane, because the file never leaves your device.
